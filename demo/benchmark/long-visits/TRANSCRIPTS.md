# Longer synthetic demo visits

These fictional visits use two synthesized voices. The speaker labels below are for reference and are omitted from the audio. Each recording can be uploaded from **New visit**.

## Same-day blood test and referral

File `lab-referral-spanish-demo.wav`. Select **Español** before uploading to demo the Spanish patient checklist.

- **Expected plan** — Complete blood count today, gynecology referral for heavy bleeding, and call the clinic if bleeding gets heavier.
- **Expected orders** — One lab and one referral, with two distinct source quotes.
- **Review for** — Low iron stays a possibility. The referral is placed, with no appointment date promised. The patient should call if bleeding gets heavier.

Clinician: Good morning. We have a few minutes to go over what brought you in and what we want to do next. What has been bothering you the most?

Patient: My periods have been heavier for about three months, and I've been really tired. I hoped the first month was just unusual, but it kept happening. By the end of my workday I feel like I have no energy left.

Clinician: When you say tired, do you mean you feel worn out, or have you actually fainted or passed out?

Patient: Worn out. I haven't fainted. I can still get through my shift, but I have less energy than I usually do, and I don't have much left when I get home. The heavier periods are the big change.

Clinician: About three months of heavier bleeding and feeling tired, and no fainting. Your pulse today is eighty-eight. We don't know the cause of your tiredness yet. Low iron is one possibility.

Patient: So when I tell my family what we talked about, should I say that I have low iron?

Clinician: Say it is one possibility. We have not confirmed that. I am ordering a complete blood count today. I am also placing a referral to gynecology for the heavy bleeding.

Patient: I can stay for the blood draw. I thought they might both happen today. Is the visit with gynecology happening this afternoon too?

Clinician: The blood test is today. The gynecology referral is being placed, and we don't have an appointment date yet.

Patient: Got it. Is there anything you want me to do if things change while I am waiting?

Clinician: Call our clinic if the bleeding gets heavier. The main steps are to get the blood test today, watch for the gynecology appointment, and call if the bleeding gets heavier.

Patient: That helps. A short list in Spanish would be easier for me to read when I get home.

Clinician: I'll put those steps in your visit instructions.

## Conditional treatment and callback

File `sore-throat-conditional.wav`.

- **Expected plan** — Hold off on tests and antibiotics today, drink fluids, and call the clinic if there is no improvement in three days.
- **Expected orders** — Zero. Amoxicillin is discussed only if a future test confirms strep.
- **Review for** — The three-day callback does not authorize medication or change into an in-person appointment.

Clinician: Hi. Before we get to the plan, tell me when the sore throat began and how it has changed.

Patient: It started two days ago. At first I noticed it when I woke up, and then by the evening it was still sore. It's about the same today. I can breathe fine. I haven't had trouble breathing.

Clinician: Today your temperature is ninety-eight point four Fahrenheit. Your throat is mildly red. This may be a viral sore throat.

Patient: My sister had strep last year and took an antibiotic. I remember the name because it sounded familiar. I thought I might need the same thing. Is that what you're doing today?

Clinician: We discussed amoxicillin as a possible treatment if a future test confirms strep. We are holding off on testing and antibiotics today.

Patient: Okay. I wanted to check whether you meant I should get the medicine if I am still sore in a few days.

Clinician: No. If you are not getting better in three days, call our clinic. That call is a check-in. Amoxicillin is something we discussed only if a future test confirms strep.

Patient: I can call on my lunch break if I need to. Sometimes I forget which things we actually decided to do after we talk through a few possibilities.

Clinician: For now, drink fluids. There is no test today and no antibiotic today. If you're not getting better in three days, call us.

Patient: So I call the clinic in three days if it isn't improving. I don't need to book another visit right now.

Clinician: Correct. The next step if you are not getting better in three days is to call our clinic.

## Knee pain, transportation and clinic follow-up

File `knee-pain-transportation.wav`. This is the longest recording.

- **Expected plan** — The clinic calls the patient tomorrow about transportation to existing physical therapy visits. The patient returns in four weeks.
- **Expected orders** — Zero. The visit discusses access to existing physical therapy, with no new referral, imaging or medication.
- **Review for** — Right knee, measured flexion of 110 degrees, no falls, two weeks of missed therapy, transportation and cost barriers, and the difference between a planned call and a confirmed ride. Omit the baseball aside.

Clinician: Before we look at your knee, I want to understand what has happened since the last visit. How are stairs going?

Patient: The right knee still hurts on stairs. I notice it going up, especially the staircase at work. At home I can take my time, but at work the stairs are in a busy hallway and I try to keep moving. I haven't fallen.

Clinician: Which knee did you say?

Patient: The right one. My left knee is fine. I mostly notice the pain on the right when I go upstairs.

Clinician: Have you been able to make it to the physical therapy visits you already had scheduled?

Patient: I missed the last two weeks. I was going when I could get a ride from my sister. Her work schedule changed, and now she can't get me there at that time. It is hard to get there by bus from where I live. I priced a ride on my phone and it costs too much for me to do that every week.

Clinician: So you missed therapy for two weeks because transportation fell through and paying for a ride is too expensive.

Patient: That's right. I had been planning to go. If I can get a ride, I want to make the visits I already have. I just can't make the trip the way I was doing it before. Do you think I have to start a new referral?

Clinician: We are talking about getting you to the physical therapy visits you already have. I am not placing a new physical therapy referral today. First, let's check how far you can bend your right knee.

Patient: Okay. Is now a good time to do that?

Clinician: Yes. Your right knee bends to one hundred ten degrees. I'm documenting ongoing right knee pain when you take the stairs.

Patient: I didn't realize you could measure the bend that exactly. I think I used those same stairs three times yesterday. I stayed late after work and listened to the baseball game on my phone while I waited for my ride.

Clinician: Did your team win?

Patient: No, they lost by one run. It was close right up to the end. Anyway, I mostly need to figure out what to do about getting to therapy.

Clinician: Our clinic will call you tomorrow about transportation options for your existing physical therapy visits. We'll talk through what options are available. I can't tell you today that a ride is booked.

Patient: So I should look out for a call tomorrow, and you'll be telling me what I can try. I don't need to call the therapy office first?

Clinician: The plan we discussed is for our clinic to call you tomorrow. We want to figure out the transportation piece for the visits you already have.

Patient: And when do you want me to come back here? It helps to have that written down before I leave.

Clinician: Come back here in four weeks. The clinic will call you tomorrow about transportation, and you should return here in four weeks.

Patient: Thanks. I can remember those two things if they're written down together.
